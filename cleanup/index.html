When a cleanup makes sense

Your books may be recorded, but are the numbers right?

Ecommerce bookkeeping can become difficult when sales happen across different platforms, fees are deducted before payouts, inventory costs aren't recorded consistently or reconciliations fall behind.

Some common situations I can help investigate and correct include:

Months of missing bookkeeping

Transactions haven't been recorded, categorized or reconciled for one or more periods.

Marketplace payouts recorded incorrectly

Amazon or Shopify deposits have been recorded without properly accounting for the underlying sales, refunds, fees or adjustments.

Unreconciled bank and clearing accounts

Deposits, transfers, settlements or outstanding balances don't agree with the supporting records.

Inventory and COGS problems

Product purchases, inventory balances or cost of goods sold may not reflect the accounting method and available stock records.

Incorrect transaction classifications

Duplicate transactions, personal expenses, sales-tax liabilities or supplier payments may have been recorded in the wrong accounts.

Books that are difficult to trust

Your reports may be incomplete or inconsistent, making it harder to understand profitability or provide reliable records to your accountant.

Not every business has all these problems. The first step is understanding what applies to yours.

The work your books need, without unnecessary extras.

Depending on the agreed project scope, cleanup and catch-up bookkeeping can involve:

Bank and credit card reconciliations

Checking Amazon, Shopify and other processor activity against the entries recorded in QuickBooks and the deposits received.

Reviewing inventory-value and COGS bookkeeping and correcting supported errors under the agreed accounting method.

Accounts receivable and payable cleanup

Reviewing outstanding invoices, supplier bills, payments and related balances where relevant.

Sales tax liability bookkeeping

Correcting the accounting classification and reconciliation of relevant sales-tax transactions. Tax filing and specialist tax advice remain separate.

Bookkeeping integrations and account structure

Reviewing existing accounting mappings and chart-of-accounts arrangements, with corrections or setup work included where specifically agreed.

Final reconciliation and review

Checking the corrected records, documenting unresolved items and preparing agreed financial reports.
